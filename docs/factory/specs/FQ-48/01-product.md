# FQ-48 — Product outcome

## The owner's problem

The owner approved a safer, less interruptive factory, but its first planned step cannot
actually begin under the factory's current rules. The owner should not need to understand
or override internal safety machinery just to recover from that planning mistake.

## Measurable success

- Work is shown as ready only when a fresh worker can genuinely begin it.
- The first recovery step needs no hidden setup from the machine that designed it.
- Recovery never asks the owner to ignore a failing safety judgment as routine.
- The owner makes at most one deliberate bootstrap authorization before the final review.
- After recovery, ordinary safe work can flow to a final decision without intermediate
  bookkeeping decisions.
- The control room states honestly whether enforcement is unavailable, partial, or active.

## Shipped announcement

Studio Baton's factory can now bootstrap itself without bypassing its own safeguards.
Ready work is genuinely executable from a fresh environment, and the owner sees only the
decisions that require human judgment.

## Deliberately not doing

- We are not weakening protection for learner data, trusted tests, or real messages.
- We are not granting an agent authority to make the final merge decision.
- We are not hiding a failed check or relabeling blocked work as ready.
- We are not changing Studio Baton product behavior.
- We are not activating live repository protection or credentials during specification.
- We are not preserving the merged sequence merely to avoid admitting it needs correction.
